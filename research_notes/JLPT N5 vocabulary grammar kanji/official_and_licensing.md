# JLPT N5: Official Content Status and Licensing of Unofficial Lists/Data Sources

Research date: 2026-10-27. Not legal advice. These notes summarize stated licenses and general principles.

## What does jlpt.jp officially say about N5 (level summary, sections, timing, scoring, absence of lists)?

### Takeaway
The official N5 descriptor is "The ability to understand some basic Japanese." The test has three timed sections: Vocabulary 20 min, Grammar·Reading 40 min, Listening 30 min (these times apply since December 2020). Scoring has two parts: Language Knowledge+Reading 0-120 with a minimum of 38, and Listening 0-60 with a minimum of 19. The total is out of 180 and the pass mark is 80. Since the 2010 revision, the organizers have published no vocabulary, kanji or grammar lists. They say such a list "was not necessarily appropriate" and point learners to the level descriptors, the test-item composition page and sample questions.

### Cited Findings
- N5 overall summary: "The ability to understand some basic Japanese." — [JLPT Level Summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html)
- N5 Reading: "One is able to read and understand typical expressions and sentences written in hiragana, katakana, and basic kanji." — [JLPT Level Summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html)
- N5 Listening: "One is able to listen and comprehend conversations about topics regularly encountered in daily life and classroom situations, and is able to pick up necessary information from short conversations spoken slowly." — [JLPT Level Summary](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/levelsummary.html)
- Test sections and times (current):
  - N5: Language Knowledge (Vocabulary) 20 min; Language Knowledge (Grammar)·Reading 40 min; Listening 30 min. Total is about 90 min.
  - N4 for comparison: 25 / 55 / 35 min.
  - Times for N4/N5 Vocabulary and Grammar·Reading changed starting with the December 2020 test (shorter times, fewer questions). Listening time can vary slightly with recording length.
  - [JLPT Test Sections and Test Times](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/testsections.html); [JLPT FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/)
- Scoring sections for N5:
  - "Language Knowledge (Vocabulary/Grammar)・Reading": 0-120, sectional pass mark 38.
  - "Listening": 0-60, sectional pass mark 19.
  - Total: 0-180, overall pass mark 80.
  - [JLPT Scoring/Results](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/guideline/results.html)
- Pass rules:
  - Candidates must meet the overall pass mark AND every sectional pass mark. "If there is even one scoring section where the score is below the sectional pass mark", the candidate fails regardless of total.
  - Skipping any section means failing.
  - Scores are scaled. The JLPT "does not total allocated points of correctly answered questions."
  - [JLPT FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/)
- On lists: the FAQ says the Test Content Specifications were discontinued with the 2010 revision because "a list of vocabulary, kanji and grammar items was not necessarily appropriate." It points instead to the "Summary of Linguistic Competence Required for Each Level", "Composition of test items" and Sample Questions. — [JLPT FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/)
- Official materials:
  - Per-level sample questions, including N5 ("Try sample questions").
  - "New Japanese-Language Proficiency Test Sample Questions".
  - The "Official Practice Workbook" (2012) and "Official Practice Workbook Vol. 2" (2018), which contain questions chosen from tests since 2010.
  - The FAQ says actual test questions are not published in full, and more sample questions will be released on an announced schedule.
  - [JLPT For Learners](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/forlearners.html); [JLPT FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/)
- Upcoming test dates listed: Sunday July 5, 2026 and Sunday December 6, 2026. — [JLPT For Learners](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/forlearners.html)
- JLPT Can-do Self-Evaluation List:
  - Built from a survey of about 65,000 examinees (Sept 2010-Dec 2011). Interim Report June 2011, Final Report October 2012 (page copyright 2012, so the material is older).
  - It summarizes what examinees who passed think they can do, across 4 skills × 20 items with colour bands by "can-do" share.
  - It is explicitly not a test syllabus.
  - The page itself did not confirm N5 item content. Check the full PDF.
  - [JLPT Can-do List](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/about/candolist.html)
- jlpt.jp's own site policy says its works are copyrighted, but they may be "reproduced, copied, adapted and translated without permission... for personal use such as individual study." — [jlpt.jp policy](https://jlpt.jp/e/policy.html)

### Inferences
- Any app labelling words as "N5" is relying on unofficial lists, mostly derived from the pre-2010 Level 4 specification. The app should say so in its UI or about screen (for example, "levels are estimates; JLPT publishes no official lists").
- The jlpt.jp personal-study permission does not obviously cover redistributing official sample questions inside a public app. Link to the official samples rather than bundling them.
- With sectional minimums and an 80/180 pass mark, both the knowledge and listening sides need practice. A listening component helps the app's readiness claims.

### Gaps
- Pre-2020 N5 section times were not given on the fetched page. Commonly cited earlier values are 25/50/30 min, but this is unverified here.
- The N5 rows of the Can-do PDF were not retrieved.

## What did the pre-2010 Test Content Specifications (出題基準) contain for Level 4, and how does it relate to N5?

### Takeaway
Old Level 4 (4級) was defined as basic grammar, about 100 kanji, about 800 words and about 150 hours of study (the first half of an elementary course). The specification also contained actual item lists. The 2010 revision mapped Level 4 to the new N5 and stopped publishing the lists. Unofficial "N5" lists such as Tanos are largely reconstructions based on the old Level 4 lists.

### Cited Findings
- Official comparison document (Japanese): "4級：初歩的な文法・漢字（100字程度）・語彙（800語程度）を習得し、簡単な会話ができ、平易な文、又は短い文章が読み書きできる能力（日本語を150時間程度学習し、初級日本語コース前半を修了したレベル）". Level 3 was about 300 kanji, about 1,500 words and about 300 hours. — [jlpt.jp comparison01.pdf](https://www.jlpt.jp/about/pdf/comparison01.pdf?mode=pc)
- The FAQ confirms that lists existed in the old Test Content Specifications and were discontinued in 2010. — [JLPT FAQ](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/faq/)
- Modern unofficial counts differ from the old figures. OpenJLPT (built from Waller's lists) has N5 = 662 words and 79 kanji. — [OpenJLPT on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/openjlpt/)

### Inferences
- Old Level 4 corresponds roughly to N5 (old 3級 was split between N4 and N3). Unofficial N5 lists therefore count fewer kanji (around 80-100) and fewer words (around 650-800).
- The 出題基準 book itself (published by the Japan Foundation/JEES, 2002 revised edition) is a copyrighted publication. Do not bundle its text. Use the CC-licensed reconstructions instead.

### Gaps
- The official comparison PDF contents were taken from a search snippet, not a full fetch.
- The exact 出題基準 Level 4 grammar item count was not found.

## Licensing of unofficial lists and data sources

### Takeaway
The cleanly bundleable sources are:
- Tanos/Waller lists: CC BY. Attribution only.
- JMdict/KANJIDIC2: CC BY-SA 4.0 plus extra EDRDG conditions (an About/Sources screen, keeping data updated).
- Tatoeba: CC BY 2.0 FR. Attribution to sentence authors.
- KanjiVG: CC BY-SA 3.0.
- OpenJLPT: CC BY-SA 4.0. This combines the above.

Share-alike (SA) applies to the data you derive. It does not apply to your app's code. JLPT Sensei, Bunpro and Kanshudo are proprietary commercial sites with no open license found. Do not scrape or bundle their content.

### Cited Findings
- **EDRDG (JMdict, KANJIDIC2, RADKFILE/KRADFILE, ENAMDICT, etc.):**
  - License: "Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike Licence (V4.0)". — [EDRDG Licence](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html)
  - Apps must acknowledge the source in documentation, publicity and website. They must include the license/documentation files or links (links are acceptable for iPhone apps) and link to the JMdict-EDICT and KANJIDIC project pages. — [EDRDG Licence](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html)
  - "Placement for apps: acknowledgement on a separate screen reached from a menu, such as 'About' or 'Sources'; a mention on the startup page is not sufficient." Websites that show dictionary words must show the acknowledgement on each screen. — [EDRDG Licence](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html)
  - Derived works may only be distributed "under the same, similar or a compatible licence." — [EDRDG Licence](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html)
  - "Software using these files does not have to be under any form of open-source licence." Commercial bundling is allowed. — [EDRDG Licence](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html)
  - Users must keep their data reasonably up to date. "Failure to keep the versions up-to-date is a violation of the licence." — [EDRDG Licence](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html)
  - Users "must NOT claim copyright over that material." — [EDRDG Licence](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html)
  - Some KANJIDIC fields are under separate copyright. For example, Jack Halpern's SKIP codes have their own CC licence and conditions. — [EDRDG Licence](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html)
  - Non-English JMdict glosses are separately copyrighted by their compilers. — [EDRDG Licence](https://www.edrdg.org/edrdg/licence.html)
- **Jisho.org:** it builds on EDRDG data, so the underlying JMdict/KANJIDIC data carries the EDRDG terms. I did not fetch Jisho's own terms, so its site-specific additions (for example, its own JLPT tags and UI) have unknown status. Get the data from EDRDG directly, not by scraping Jisho. (Inference; see Gaps.)
- **Tanos (Jonathan Waller):**
  - Downstream projects consistently state that the lists are licensed under Creative Commons "BY" by Jonathan Waller.
  - — [Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary](https://github.com/Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary); [hanabira.org repo](https://github.com/tristcoil/hanabira.org); [tanos-japanese-word-books README](https://git.pvv.ntnu.no/oysteikt/tanos-japanese-word-books/raw/branch/main/README.md); [Japanese.io sources](https://www.japanese.io/sources/)
  - tanos.co.uk/jlpt did not resolve (DNS failure) on 2026-10-27. One repo describes the site as "now defunct". The licence text could not be verified on the original site. — [tanos-japanese-word-books](https://git.pvv.ntnu.no/oysteikt/tanos-japanese-word-books)
  - A forum user reports some wrong definitions in Tanos word lists. — [forum.japandict.com](https://forum.japandict.com/d/22/5)
- **Tatoeba:** text sentences are CC BY 2.0 FR. "using, reusing, modifying and distributing the sentence is only allowed if the name of the author is cited." Some sentences are CC0. Audio has a BY baseline but may carry ND/SA or other licenses, so check each recording. Conditions may be added but never removed. — [Tatoeba Terms of Use](https://tatoeba.org/en/terms_of_use)
- **KanjiVG:** "released under the Creative Commons Attribution-Share Alike 3.0 license". Copyright is held by Ulrich Apel. — [KanjiVG](https://kanjivg.tagaini.net/)
- **JLPT Sensei:** its terms-of-service page returned no usable text, and no open license was found. — [jlptsensei.com/terms-of-service](https://jlptsensei.com/terms-of-service/) (fetch returned empty)
- **Bunpro:** no published open license was found. A community thread asking how Bunpro materials are licensed got only an informal reply: copying large sections or packaging many sentences would be a problem, while a few scattered items are fine. — [Bunpro community thread](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/how-are-the-materials-on-bunpro-licensed/166870)
- General principle: scraping in breach of a site's terms can be a breach of contract, separately from any copyright claim. — [Monolith Law](https://monolith.law/en/general-corporate/scraping-datacollection-law)
- **GitHub/PyPI datasets:**
  - **OpenJLPT 0.1.0** (released 2026-07-21): CC BY-SA 4.0 for data and code. Sources are Waller (levels), KANJIDIC2 (kanji details) and Tatoeba (examples, covering about 89% of words). Attribution is in NOTICE.md. — [OpenJLPT](https://pypi.org/project/openjlpt/)
  - **Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary:** repo licence MIT. The data is from Tanos (CC BY), so attribution to Waller is still required for the data. — [Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary](https://github.com/Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary)
  - **scriptin/jmdict-simplified:** derived JMdict/Kanjidic JSON under the EDRDG licence / CC BY-SA 4.0. Project files are CC BY-SA 4.0 and the npm packages are MIT. — [jmdict-simplified](https://github.com/scriptin/jmdict-simplified)
  - **Smallsan/jlpt_kanji_json_msgpack:** licence follows the original dictionary sources. — [GitHub](https://github.com/Smallsan/jlpt_kanji_json_msgpack)
  - **AnchorI/jlpt-kanji-dictionary:** no licence was seen in the snippet. — [GitHub](https://github.com/AnchorI/jlpt-kanji-dictionary)
  - **KanaDojo** lists vocab as "Public Domain / Community Maintained", which is not a formal licence. — [KanaDojo credits](https://kanadojo.com/credits)

### Inferences
- Practical attribution bundle for the app's "About / Sources" screen:
  - "JLPT level data: Jonathan Waller, tanos.co.uk (CC BY)"
  - "Dictionary data: JMdict/KANJIDIC2 © EDRDG, CC BY-SA 4.0" with links to the EDRDG licence and project pages
  - "Example sentences: Tatoeba (tatoeba.org), CC BY 2.0 FR", plus a per-sentence author credit or a link to each sentence
  - "Stroke order: KanjiVG © Ulrich Apel, CC BY-SA 3.0"
- Share-alike: if the app ships a modified JSON derived from JMdict, KANJIDIC2 or KanjiVG, that data file must be under CC BY-SA (or a compatible licence). The app code can stay MIT or any other licence. EDRDG says this explicitly, and keeping data files separate from code is the usual practice.
- A combined N5 dataset that joins Waller levels (BY) with JMdict glosses (BY-SA) ends up BY-SA overall. That is why OpenJLPT is BY-SA.
- The EDRDG "keep data updated" clause applies to a bundled app. Plan a periodic data refresh, for example by pulling jmdict-simplified's weekly releases.
- For grammar, no open-licensed structured N5 grammar dataset was found. Grammar point names (for example 〜てください) are uncopyrightable facts. Explanations and example sentences written by Bunpro, JLPT Sensei or Kanshudo are copyrighted expression, so write your own explanations and use Tatoeba sentences.

## Can a study app include Genki textbook vocabulary grouped by lesson?

### Takeaway
Under US law (Feist), individual words and their meanings are facts that copyright does not protect. A compilation is protected only for its original selection and arrangement. A textbook's per-lesson vocabulary selection and ordering is arguably exactly such an editorial selection, so reproducing "Genki Lesson 3 vocab" wholesale carries real (if thin-copyright) risk. Copying Genki's translations, notes or audio carries more risk. The Japan Times sells an official GENKI Vocab Cards app, which means the lesson-grouped vocab is a commercial product it actively monetizes. Community Anki decks split by lesson exist widely, but that shows tolerance, not legality.

### Cited Findings
- Compilation copyright covers "the creative choice of what data to include or exclude, the order and style in which the information is presented", not the underlying facts. A later compiler may use the facts "so long as the competing work does not feature the same selection and arrangement." — [Wikipedia: Feist v. Rural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feist_Publications,_Inc._v._Rural_Telephone_Service_Co.); [Copyright in compilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_in_compilation)
- The US Copyright Office will register an author's list of their "50 favorite restaurants" (a creative selection) but not a list of all restaurants in an area. — [Manatt on Copyright Office compilations](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/intellectual-property-law/mea-culpa-u-s-copyright-office-clarifies-definit)
- The Japan Times publishes the official "GENKI Vocab Cards" app, covering about 1,200 words. — [appgoblin listing](https://appgoblin.info/apps/785207187)
- Community lesson-split Genki 2nd/3rd edition Anki decks are hosted by a fan site and were created by an anonymous AnkiWeb user. — [genki.sixfalls.me Anki decks](https://genki.sixfalls.me/help/anki-decks/)
- A Mnemosyne deck covers the 602 words of Genki I. — [Mnemosyne](https://mnemosyne-proj.org/cards/genki-i-vocabulary)
- No reports of Japan Times takedowns against such decks were found. — (search, no result)

### Inferences
- Lower-risk patterns:
  1. Let users tag or import their own lesson lists, as a "bring your own deck" feature.
  2. Map your own CC-licensed vocab (JMdict glosses) to lesson numbers via a minimal index, such as lesson → list of word IDs, without Genki's translations, order within the lesson, notes or audio. This is still a reproduction of the selection, so it is a grey area.
  3. Organize by JLPT level or by your own topical curriculum and say "compatible with Genki" without reproducing the lists. Note that "Genki" is a Japan Times trademark, so avoid implying endorsement.
- Japan (Copyright Act Art. 12 on compilations, Art. 12-2 on databases) and the EU (sui generis database right) also protect selection/arrangement, and the EU right adds protection for substantial investment. This is a general principle and was not verified against sources in this session.
- For an open-source repo on GitHub, a DMCA takedown of a bundled Genki list is the realistic consequence, and it would disrupt the project. Keep any Genki mapping out of the core dataset.

### Gaps
- No case law was found on textbook vocabulary lists specifically.
- No Japan Times statement on fan decks or apps was found.
- Japanese and EU law was not sourced in this session.

## Best machine-readable datasets for N5 vocab, kanji and grammar, and their schemas

### Takeaway
OpenJLPT is currently the most turnkey option. It offers JSON per level, CSV and SQLite for vocab and kanji, and is CC BY-SA 4.0, but it is new (v0.1.0, July 2026) and has no grammar yet. Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary gives raw Tanos levels as JSON/CSV. jmdict-simplified gives full dictionary JSON (no JLPT tags) to join against. For grammar there is no good open dataset, so you will need to author one.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenJLPT:**
  - Vocab fields: `word`, `reading`, `meanings`, `level`, `examples[{ja, en}]`.
  - Kanji fields: `character`, `level`, `strokes`, `grade`, `freq`, `onyomi`, `kunyomi`, `meanings`.
  - Files: `data/json/vocab/n5.json`, `vocab-n5.csv`, `kanji-n5.csv`, and `openjlpt.sqlite` (tables `vocab` and `kanji`).
  - Python and npm TypeScript loaders. Schema is versioned and validated in CI.
  - N5 = 662 words, 79 kanji. Grammar is on the roadmap only.
  - [OpenJLPT](https://pypi.org/project/openjlpt/)
- **Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary:**
  - `JLPT_vocab_ALL.json` maps a word or reading key to a list of `{reading, level}` objects (for example "挨拶" → reading あいさつ, level 3).
  - `JLPT_kanji_ALL.json` maps a kanji to an integer level (for example "気": 5).
  - CSV rows are word,reading. It has "_cleaned" variants.
  - [Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary](https://github.com/Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary)
- **jmdict-simplified:**
  - JSON with self-contained entries, no nulls (empty arrays instead) and readable field names.
  - Typed in `node/packages/jmdict-simplified-types/index.ts`.
  - Weekly automated releases (Mondays).
  - No JLPT levels. Its commonness markers are news1/ichi1/spec1/spec2/gai1.
  - [jmdict-simplified](https://github.com/scriptin/jmdict-simplified)

### Inferences
- Suggested pipeline:
  1. Take N5 membership from OpenJLPT, or from Tanos via Bluskyo.
  2. Join on word+reading to the JMdict entry ID from jmdict-simplified, which gives stable IDs and fresh glosses (and satisfies the EDRDG update clause).
  3. Add KANJIDIC2 kanji data and KanjiVG stroke SVGs.
  4. Add Tatoeba sentences with author IDs stored for attribution.
- Store your own derived data as a separate CC BY-SA file with a NOTICE file.

### Gaps
- The exact field names of jmdict-simplified's word schema were not fetched. They are in its docs and TS types.
- The Tanos N5 word count was not confirmed from the source.
- No open N5 grammar dataset was found.
