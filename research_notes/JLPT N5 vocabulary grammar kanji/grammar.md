## Q1. What are the main N5 grammar lists, and how many points does each have?

### Takeaway
There has been no official JLPT grammar list since the 2010 revision, so every list is a third-party reconstruction. Sizes run from about 40 (Tanos/Wikibooks, based on old official specs) to about 84 (JLPT Sensei) and about 130 (Bunpro, which splits points finely). "80-130 points" is a fair range for the modern study lists.

### Cited Findings
- JLPT Sensei's N5 grammar list has 84 entries over 3 pages: 40 + 40 + 4. Items 81-84 are はどうですか, や, よ and より～ほうが. — [JLPT Sensei p1](https://jlptsensei.com/jlpt-n5-grammar-list/), [p2](https://jlptsensei.com/jlpt-n5-grammar-list/page/2/), [p3](https://jlptsensei.com/jlpt-n5-grammar-list/page/3/)
- Bunpro's grammar index shows about 132 N5 grammar points over 10 lessons. This is a manual tally from the fetched page, so it may be off by a few. It showed about 186 N4 points, but that figure comes from a partial read of the page. — [Bunpro grammar points](https://bunpro.jp/grammar_points)
- Wikibooks' "JLPT Guide/JLPT N5 Grammar" lists 40 points, from です to てから. It says the list was compiled by Jonathan Waller (Tanos) and is "based on several years' old official test specifications." It warns that the test changes slightly from year to year. — [Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/JLPT_Guide/JLPT_N5_Grammar)
  - The 40 points: も, で, に/へ, に, を, は, と, や, から, ので, てください, てもいいです, てはいけません, ないでください, ましょう/ましょうか, ませんか, があります, がいます, ている, たことがある, まだ～ていません, てから, まえに, にいく, stem+たいです, たり～たり, つもり, のがすき, のがじょうず, のがへた, のほうが～より, のなかで～がいちばん, く/になる, すぎる, ほうがいい, なくちゃいけない, んです, でしょう, and similar.
- Bunpro community members say there is no official list of which grammar points appear on the test, and that resources differ for this reason. — [Bunpro forum](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/is-there-an-official-list-of-grammar-points/49920), [Bunpro forum 2](https://community.bunpro.jp/t/list-of-grammar-points-covered-by-level/74198)
- Other counts that circulate:
  - Gokigen: "71 Essential Points"
  - Migaku: about 60 points
  - GyanMirai: about 80-100 points

  All three are reported through a search summary. — [Gokigen](https://blog.gokigen.jp/japanese-grammar/n5/), [Migaku](https://migaku.com/blog/japanese/jlpt-grammar-points), [GyanMirai](https://www.gyanmirai.com/jlpt/jlpt-n5/grammar-study)
- 3A Network publishes a commercial N5 grammar workbook, "JLPT Bunpo N5 Pointo & Purakutisu." — [3A Network](https://www.3anet.co.jp/np/en/books/3858/)

### Inferences
- How finely a list splits its points explains most of the size difference. Bunpro gives separate entries to things like この/その/あの, ここ/そこ/あそこ, い-adj past and な-adj past. Tanos merges particles and core conjugation into a few entries.
- Licensing: JLPT Sensei, Bunpro and Tanos content is copyrighted or site-specific. Use these lists as references for which points to include, and write your own glosses and examples. The examples in this file are original.

### Gaps
- Tanos's own page count could not be verified because the site did not resolve.
- I did not fetch the N5 counts for Nihongo no Mori or the Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar (DBJG). DBJG is a reference dictionary organised by entry, not by JLPT level, so it has no "N5 list" as such.
- Bunpro's count is approximate.

## Q2. Consolidated N5 grammar list, grouped by category, with glosses

### Takeaway
This is a union of the JLPT Sensei 84-point list and the Tanos/Wikibooks 40-point list, plus core conjugation that every list assumes. It comes to about 95 items in 9 categories.

### Cited Findings
Unless noted, items come from the JLPT Sensei 84-point list ([p1](https://jlptsensei.com/jlpt-n5-grammar-list/), [p2](https://jlptsensei.com/jlpt-n5-grammar-list/page/2/), [p3](https://jlptsensei.com/jlpt-n5-grammar-list/page/3/)) or the Tanos-derived [Wikibooks list](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/JLPT_Guide/JLPT_N5_Grammar). Glosses are my own wording.

**A. Particles**
- は: topic marker ("as for X")
- が: subject marker; also "but" between clauses
- を: direct object marker
- に: point in time, destination, location of existence, target or recipient
- へ: direction ("toward")
- で: place of an action; means or tool ("by, with"); also "among / within" in の中で
- と: "and" between nouns (an exhaustive list); "with" a person
- や: "and" between nouns (a non-exhaustive list: "A, B, and so on")
- の: possessive or linking ("X's", "X of Y"); also a pronoun for a noun ("the one")
- も: "also, too"; with a negative, "not even / neither"
- か: question marker; A か B means "A or B"
- から: "from" (time or place); "because" (after a clause)
- まで: "until, up to"
- より: "than"
- だけ: "only, just"
- ね: sentence end, seeking agreement ("right?")
- よ: sentence end, asserting or informing ("you know")
- なあ: sentence end, exclamation (JLPT Sensei lists it)

**B. Copula and existence**
- です / だ: "is, am, are" (polite / plain)
- じゃない / ではない (じゃありません): "is not"
- でした / だった: "was"
- でしょう / だろう: "probably"; でしょう? is also "right?"
- があります / ある: there is or there exists (inanimate things)
- がいます / いる: there is or there exists (animate beings)

**C. Demonstratives and question words** (core; some lists fold these in)
- これ/それ/あれ/どれ: "this / that / that over there / which" (pronouns)
- この/その/あの/どの + noun: "this / that / that ~ over there / which ~"
- ここ/そこ/あそこ/どこ: "here / there / over there / where"
- だれ, なに/なん, いつ, いくら, どう, どんな, どうして, どうやって: who, what, when, how much, how, what kind of, why, how (by what means)
- なにか / なにも + negative; どこか / どこにも + negative: "something / nothing", "somewhere / nowhere"

**D. Verb forms**
- ます forms (ます/ません/ました/ませんでした): polite present/future and past, affirmative and negative
- Dictionary form, ない form, た form, なかった form: the plain (short) forms
- て form: links actions ("and then"), and is the base for many patterns
- ている: an action in progress, or a resulting state (けっこんしている, "is married")
- てある: a state that results from a deliberate action ("has been done")
- たい: "want to (do)"; がほしい: "want (a thing)"
- ましょう: "let's"; ましょうか: "shall we / shall I?"; ませんか: "won't you? / why don't we?"
- に行く / に来る (verb stem + に): "go/come in order to do"
- たことがある: "have done before" (experience)
- たり～たりする: "do things like A and B"
- つもり: "intend to"
- なる: "become" (Adj-く/に + なる)
- 方 (かた): "the way of doing" (stem + かた)
- すぎる: "too much / overly"

**E. Requests, permission, prohibition, obligation**
- てください: "please do"
- ないでください: "please don't"
- をください: "please give me X"
- てもいい(です): "it's okay to / may I"
- てはいけない: "must not"
- ちゃいけない / じゃいけない: casual "must not"
- なくてはいけない / なくてはならない / なければいけない / なくちゃ / ないといけない: "must / have to"
- なくてもいい: "don't have to"
- ほうがいい: "had better" (た-form + ほうがいい; ない-form + ほうがいい)

**F. Adjectives**
- い-adjectives: present, negative (くない), past (かった), past negative (くなかった), adverb form (く), て-form (くて)
- な-adjectives: な + noun, じゃない, だった, に (adverb), で (て-form)
- のが好き / のが上手 / のが下手: "like / be good at / be bad at doing"
- がすき / きらい / じょうず / へた: preferences and skill with が

**G. Comparison and degree**
- AはBより～: "A is more ~ than B"
- BよりAのほうが～: "A is more ~ than B" (literally, "compared with B, A's side is more ~")
- ～の中で…がいちばん: "among ~, … is the most"
- いちばん: "the most"
- とても, あまり + negative, ぜんぜん + negative: degree adverbs (some lists include them)
- はどうですか: "how about ~? / how is ~?"

**H. Clause connection and time**
- から: "because" (subjective reason)
- ので: "because, since" (softer, more objective)
- が / けど / けれども: "but, although"
- てから: "after doing"
- まえに: "before (doing / noun)"
- とき: "when"
- ないで: "without doing"
- まだ / もう; まだ～ていません: "still / already; not yet"
- そして, それから, でも, しかし: sentence-initial "and, and then, but, however"
- いつも, いっしょに: "always", "together" (JLPT Sensei lists these as grammar items)

**I. Explanation, nominalisation, other**
- んです / のです: explanatory or emphatic ("it's that…")
- にする: "decide on / choose X"
- お / ご: honorific or polite prefix
- Noun modification by a clause (relative clause, e.g. 本を読んでいる人, "the person reading a book"): Genki teaches this in L9. Tanos/Sensei treat it as assumed core grammar.

### Inferences
- Not every list includes these: なあ, しかし, いつも, いっしょに, そして, お/ご, なくてはならない. They come from JLPT Sensei's broader 84-item list, and the Tanos 40-point list omits them. Some are really vocabulary.
- A minimal "core" N5 set for an app is the Tanos 40 plus the conjugation basics (sections D and F) and demonstratives (C). That comes to roughly 60-70 items.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Bunpro's exact split or wording item by item. Only the count was read.

## Q3. Genki I (3rd ed.) lesson-by-lesson grammar; N5 points not covered; Genki I points that are N4+

### Takeaway
Genki I (L1-12) covers almost all common N5 grammar, and some of it goes slightly into N4 territory, mainly the short-form quotation patterns と思う and と言っていました. Most of the N5 gaps are covered later, in Genki II. The common verdict on learner forums is "Genki I ≈ N5 grammar."

### Cited Findings
- The Genki 3rd-edition dialogue titles for Genki I are:
  - L1 New Friends
  - L2 Shopping
  - L3 Making a Date
  - L4 The First Date
  - L5 A Trip to Okinawa
  - L6 A Day in Robert's Life
  - L7 Family Picture
  - L8 Barbecue
  - L9 Kabuki
  - L10 Winter Vacation Plans
  - L11 After the Vacation
  - L12 Feeling Ill

  The official syllabus PDF is at bookclub2.japantimes.co.jp. — [Genki 3rd ed. intro](https://genki3.japantimes.co.jp/en/intro/), [Syllabus PDF](https://bookclub2.japantimes.co.jp/download/files/genki3/genki-3rd_syllabus_E.pdf)
- The Japan Times describes the series as covering "almost all of the basic sentence patterns for beginners," from desu/masu through honorific, passive, causative and causative-passive. Those advanced forms are in Genki II. — [Genki intro](https://genki3.japantimes.co.jp/en/intro/)
- One retailer says the 3rd edition keeps the same grammar points as the 2nd edition, with revised explanations, and adds Culture Notes. — reported via search summary of a [retailer listing](https://shop.whiterabbitjapan.com/collections/bundle/products/genki-ii-3rd-edition-complete-set)

### Inferences
**N5 points (from the JLPT Sensei list) not taught as grammar points in Genki I.** These are inferred by comparing the Sensei list with the decoded syllabus. Where I place a point in Genki II, that is from memory and not verified here.
- てある: Genki II, about L21
- てから: Genki II
- まえに: Genki II
- とき: Genki II, about L16
- ないで (without doing): Genki II
- なくてもいい: Genki II
- 方 (かた): Genki II
- にする: Genki II
- ないといけない and なくてはならない: Genki uses なければいけない/なきゃ instead
- ちゃいけない / じゃいけない (casual prohibition): Genki II
- だけ: appears as vocabulary or in later lessons
- まで: appears in vocabulary or expressions
- のが上手/下手: Genki I has のが好き/きらい; 上手/下手 with のが is an easy extension
- お/ご honorific prefix: formally taught with honorifics in Genki II
- がほしい: Genki II, about L14
- はどうですか and なあ: in Genki as expressions only
- Learners using Genki I for N5 should add short supplements for てから, まえに, とき, ないで, なくてもいい, てある, だけ/まで, and がほしい.

**Genki I points that most lists rate N4 or borderline.** This is my assessment, based on these points being absent from JLPT Sensei's 84-item N5 list.
- Short form + と思います: "I think that…" (L8)
- と言っていました: "said that…" (L8, L9)
- The full short-form system in casual speech (L8, L9)
- Relative-clause noun modification (L9): treated as core by some lists and N4 by others
- Adjective/Noun + の as a pronoun (L10: 「安いのがいい」, "the cheap one")
- ている for a resultant state (L7) and でしょう are on the N5 list, so they are not beyond N5.
